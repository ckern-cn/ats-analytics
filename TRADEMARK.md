# Brand and trademark policy

This is a project policy, not legal advice.

## Code vs. brand

The **source code** is free software under the MIT License (see `LICENSE`).
That means you may use, modify, and redistribute the code, including for
commercial purposes, as long as you keep the copyright and license notice.

The **brand** is separate. The ATS name, official logo, icons, favicon,
official domain `ats.ckern.cn`, and official release artifacts are project
brand assets and are **not** granted to you by the MIT License (see `NOTICE`).
"ATS" is a generic acronym; this policy covers the project's official visual
identity and official distribution channels, not the letters themselves.

## Official channels

Official releases are published only by the maintainer:

- GitHub: <https://github.com/ckern-cn/ats-analytics>
- Gitee: <https://gitee.com/mywliu/ats-analytics>

Only artifacts published from these channels are official. If you get a build
elsewhere, it is an unofficial build unless the maintainer says otherwise.

## Forks

You are welcome to fork the code. If you distribute a modified version, you
must:

1. use a different project name;
2. replace the official logo, icons and favicon with your own;
3. not use `ats.ckern.cn` or the official release channels as your own;
4. not state or imply that your fork is official, endorsed, or maintained by
   the ATS maintainer;
5. keep the MIT copyright and license notices in the source.

You may accurately say that your project is “based on ATS” or “a fork of ATS”,
provided it is clear that it is not the official project.

## Permission

If you want to use the official name or logo for a talk, article, package,
distribution, or other non-fork use, ask the maintainer first through the
repository profile. Reasonable references to the project name for describing
the origin of the work are fine.

## Why this split?

The project is meant to be useful and free to run, study, modify, and share.
The brand policy exists so users can tell official builds and support from
third-party builds, and so the maintainer keeps control of the project's
identity and official release process.
